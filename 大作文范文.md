Essays 1：教育类 / 科技与社会类（混合话题）
Some people argue that because the Internet makes it so easy for children to
access facts, schools should not focus on teaching facts. Instead, they should
focus on developing children's skills and potential, and their relationships
with other people.
To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

As information is now so accessible online, it can be argued that schools should
move away from fact-based teaching and towards work on skills and relationship
building. I completely agree with this idea.

There are two main reasons why I believe that school teachers should no longer
focus on presenting factual information to their students. Firstly, there is simply no
need for teachers to create lessons that are already available in multimedia format
on the Internet. YouTube, for example, contains videos that cover the full range of
school subjects and lesson topics. Secondly, young people are so adept at using
websites and search engines that they can be trusted to do their own online fact-
finding. In my opinion, this research could be done as homework, which would
remove the need for the teaching of facts in the classroom.

A shift away from the traditional style of teaching would allow schools to build their
students' practical and social skills. Useful practical skills might include online
research, presentations, website design, and even computer programming.
Competence in these areas is in high demand in today's job market. With regard to
social or relationship skills, a modern approach to education would put more
emphasis on group projects that require teamwork, negotiation and the delegation of
tasks. This way of teaching could develop students' emotional intelligence and
leadership qualities, and help all children, regardless of academic ability, to reach
their potential.

In conclusion, I believe that classroom teaching should prioritise skills and
interpersonal relationships, while factual knowledge can be studied at home via the
Internet.

Essays 2： 文化与休闲类
Some people say that music is a good way of bringing people of different
cultures and ages together.
To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

It is often said that music has the power to unite and connect people, regardless of
their cultural backgrounds or ages. I completely agree with this view, and will give
my reasons below.

Music can certainly reach across cultural and national boundaries and bring people
together. Perhaps the best example of this would be the Live Aid concerts that took
place back in the 1980s, and which were broadcast to a global audience. Two live
events were held simultaneously in the UK and the US, and the objective was to
raise funds for famine relief in Ethiopia. The concerts were a huge success, both in
terms of the number of people around the world who watched them and their impact
on international public awareness of the famine. They demonstrated, I believe, that
music truly is the planet's global language.

Just as it transcends cultures, music also has the ability to connect people from
different generations. Regardless of age, we can all enjoy a memorable melody, a
strong rhythm or a beautiful singing voice, and the best songs seem to have the
same magical effect on all of us. This would explain why televised music
competitions, such as 'The X Factor' or 'The Voice', , are such popular prime-time
shows. These programmes attract incredibly broad audiences because singing and
popular songs appeal to children, parents and grandparents alike. I would argue that
no other form of entertainment can bring families together in this way.

In conclusion, I believe that music is unique in its capacity to create shared
experiences between people, irrespective of culture and age.

Essays 3：职场与社会类
First impressions are important. Some people think that doing well in
interviews is the key to securing a good job.
To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Most people try to make a good impression when they meet others, especially in
work situations. While I accept that it is important to come across well in a job
interview, I disagree with the idea that interview performance is the decisive factor in
the recruitment process.

There are two main reasons why candidates seek to make a good first impression in
job interviews. Firstly, interviews are a great opportunity for applicants to
demonstrate competence to a potential employer. They can do this by answering
questions well and by describing relevant work experience. Secondly, interviewees
realise that it is important to come across well on a personal level, in order to build
rapport with the interviewer and show that they can become part of his or her team.
This is why positive body language, including eye contact, smiling and a firm
handshake, is essential in the interview situation.

However, I would argue that qualifications and experience are more important than
interview performance. Qualifications are an essential prerequisite for employment
because they show whether or not the candidate has the relevant knowledge for the
position. They can also tell the interviewer something about the applicant's
character, in the sense that good grades are usually achieved through persistent
focus and hard work. Similarly, a person's previous experience tells the employer
how prepared he or she is to start work. For example, a teacher who has taught in
schools for several years is unlikely to need the classroom management training that
a recent graduate may require.

In conclusion, I believe that qualifications and experience are the keys to securing a
job, although good first impressions at the interview stage can also help.

Essays 4：社会与家庭类
Caring for children is probably the most important job in any society. Because
of this, all mothers and fathers should be required to take a course that
prepares them to be good parents.
To what extent do you agree or disagree with this view?

It is true that parents shoulder a huge responsibility and that raising children is by no
means an easy task. However, I completely disagree with the idea that we should
therefore force al mothers and fathers to attend parenting courses.

In my opinion, the idea that al future parents should take a parenthood preparation
course is completely impractical. Many prospective parents have jobs and busy
schedules, and they may not be willing or able to attend regular parenting classes.
This raises the question of whether those who missed the classes, or perhaps
refused to attend, would be punished. I believe that it would be wrong to do this, and
it would therefore be impossible to enforce the idea of compulsory training for
parents. Besides, even if parents could be forced to attend, I doubt that people
would agree on what good parenting entails, and so it would be difficult to create a
parenting course to suit everyone.

As well as being impractical, I would argue that training courses for parents are
unnecessary. Mothers and fathers have been raising children without any formal
help or official interference for thousands of years. Parenting skills are learnt from
family members, friends, neighbours and the surrounding culture. Perhaps more
importantly, adults learn to be good parents by instinct, by trial and error, and by
getting to know their own children; for example, a good parent will try different
strategies when faced with a badly-behaved child, and will gradually develop an
understanding of what works to correct the behaviour. None of this requires the
intervention of a taught course.

In conclusion, while compulsory parenting lessons might seem like a good idea, I
believe that such a scheme would be unworkable and largely pointless.

Essays 5：政府政策与健康类
Many manufactured food and drink products contain high levels of sugar,
which causes many health problems. Sugary products should be made more
expensive to encourage people to consume less sugar.
Do you agree or disagree?

It is true that the excess sugar in processed foods and beverages contributes to a
range of illnesses and health issues. However, I completely disagree with the idea
that raising the price of sugary products is the best way to address this problem.

In my opinion, there is absolutely no need to make certain products more expensive
in order to highlight their sugar content. People these days are well aware that soft
drinks, such as cola, and sweet foods, like cakes and biscuits, are high in sugar.
Most of us also know that sugar is added to cooking sauces and takeaway meals to
improve the taste, and we understand that such foods are less healthy than home-
cooked meals. The average person sees sugary products as treats for special
occasions, and is able to consume them in moderation. Therefore, I believe that we
should trust adults to make their own health decisions, rather than trying to control
their eating habits through government measures.

I also believe that the proposed price increase would be unfair. I predict that this idea
would have unintended consequences, especially for less wealthy members of
society. For example, poorer parents may not be able to afford cakes, sweets and
fizzy drinks for their children's birthday parties. By contrast, wealthy people would
not even notice the price change, and would continue to buy whatever foods and
drinks they liked. So, instead of encouraging positive eating habits, an increase in
the price of sugary products could end up being a discriminatory measure that turns
simple treats into luxuries.

In conclusion, I consider a price rise for products containing high levels of sugar to
be an unnecessary and unfair proposal.

Many people believe that all children should be given the opportunity to learn a
musical instrument. I completely agree with this idea, for a variety of reasons.

melodies, rhythms and songs on an instrument. Such enjoyment can be even more
intense when people play music together in a band, or perhaps even in a full

mastery of a full song or piece of music.

every child. For example, they could invest in a set of electronic keyboards, some
inexpensive guitars, and perhaps some woodwind and percussion instruments.
children could then choose an instrument and receive a certain amount of 
scheduled tuition each week.

In conclusion, I would like all children to enjoy being able to play a musical
instrument, and I believe that this is an achievable goal for schools to have.

Essays 7：语言、文化与移民类
Living in a country where you have to speak a foreign language can cause
serious social problems, as well as practical problems.
To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?

Life in a country where a different language is spoken can be challenging. I agree
with the idea that people who put themselves in this situation should prepare for
significant social and practical problems.

The language barrier and differences in behaviour are the main social problems for
anyone living in a foreign-language environment. Most migrants are not proficient in the
new language, which makes meeting local people and making friends difficult. These
challenges can have a negative effect on an individual's confidence, and in some cases
lead to feelings of loneliness, isolation or alienation. Another potential social
problem is that new arrivals may offend locals inadvertently if they are unaware of
cultural expectations, norms, differences regarding politeness. In Britain, for example, it is easy for non-native
speakers to sound rude if they forget to say 'please' and 'thank you'.

Two practical problems for non-native speakers are finding accommodation and
securing work. When searching for a place to live, migrants face language
difficulties, which range from speaking to estate agents to understanding contracts.
They are at a particular disadvantage when it comes to dealing with disputes with
landlords, who may exploit _them by charging more. With regard to finding work,
employers are often reluctant to hire staff who are not fluent, proficient in the local language.
They may worry that overseas workers will struggle to understand phone
conversations, which could lead to miscommunication with customers.

In conclusion, living in a foreign-language culture is undoubtedly difficult, both socially
and for practical reasons.

with patients' families, and providing general care and comfort to people who are il.

jobs. Many would-be nurses may choose other professions rather than incur large
student debts that will be difficult to repay. Free training would at least remove this
obstacle and make a career in nursing more desirable.

In conclusion, I believe that free tuition is a good way to show the value that we
place on nurses, and to incentivise more people to enter the nursing profession.

pictures. Some people say that these pictures are more effective than words.
To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

Some people believe that images are better than words for communicating news
stories. I would only partly agree with this, because I believe that words can be more
powerful in certain situations.

On the one hand, I agree that pictures are often more impactful than written text in
the context of a news article or broadcast. In some cases, a single image can
capture the essence of an event and provoke an emotional response much more
effectively than a description would. For example, I remember being immediately
shocked when I saw footage of the tsunami that hit Japan in 2011. Images can also
help to convey information that is difficult to put into words, such as the scale of a
disaster or the enormity of a protest. A recent example of this was the devastation
caused by the Turkey-Syria earthquake, which would have been impossible to
describe using words alone.

than a picture. Newspapers regularly lead with dramatic or witty headlines, rather
than a photograph, and readers may remember these long after an image is
forgotten. For example, I still associate the phrase "Yes, we can" with Barack
Obama's 2008 election campaign, and several news outlets used that slogan to
great effect in their headlines. Longer passages of text can also be extremely
powerful, especially when a particular news story is complex. This was the case in
early 2020, when we al wanted to learn as much as possible about the new
coronavirus, and pictures alone could not communicate all the details of what was
happening.

In conclusion, while news pictures can grab our attention and convey ideas very
quickly, words can sometimes be more memorable and informative.

Essays 10：**教育与现代科技类**
As computers are being used more and more in education, there will soon be
no role for the teacher in the classroom.
To what extent do you agree or disagree?

It is true that computers have become an essential tool for teachers and students in
all areas of education. However, while computers are extremely useful, I do not
agree with the idea that they could soon replace teachers completely.

On the one hand, the use of information technology (IT) should certainly be an
integral part of every school curriculum. Schools must teach a full range of IT skills,
from online research to report writing, in order to prepare their students for the
working world. At the same time, technology is a powerful tool to engage students.
The use of websites or online videos can make lessons much more interesting, and
many students are more motivated to do homework using online resources.
Computers also make it easier for students to study at their own pace.

However, all of the activities described above require some input from a human
teacher. School pupils need to be guided by a real person who can structure their
learning programmes and support their use of IT. A computer can never really get to
know a student and will not be able to respond to problems, answer questions or
give precise feedback. Consequently, without the presence of a human teacher,
students may become confused and unmotivated. This is exactly what happened
during the 2020 pandemic when both teachers and students realised that video
lessons were a poor substitute for the classroom experience.

In conclusion, computers can help in the classroom, but they will never supplant
human teachers.

often argued that these are the best people to talk to teenagers about the

completely agree with the idea that allowing such people to speak to teenagers

speak from experience. Reformed offenders can tell young people about how they
became involved in crime, the dangers of a criminal lifestyle, and what life in prison
is really like. They can also dispel any ideas that teenagers may have about
criminals leading glamorous lives. While adolescents are often indifferent to the
guidance given by older people, I imagine that most of them would be extremely

be much less effective. One option would be for police officers to visit schools and
talk to young people. This could be useful in terms of informing teens about what
happens to lawbreakers when they are caught, but young people are often reluctant
to take advice from figures of authority. A second option would be for school
teachers to speak to their students about crime, but I doubt that students would see
teachers as credible sources of information about this topic. Finally, educational films
might be informative, but there would be no opportunity for young people to interact
and ask questions.

In conclusion, I fully support the view that people who have turned their lives around
after serving a prison sentence could help to deter teenagers from committing
crimes.

To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?

road infrastructures. I completely disagree with this idea, for a variety of reasons.

continual investment is required to maintain and update them. Roads should be
prioritised because they are used by every individual citizen in any given country. We
all live on or near a street, and our daily movements rely on our road networks being
extensive and in good condition. Furthermore, streets and highways serve not only
drivers of private cars, but also buses, taxis, cyclists, and a whole range of delivery
services for shops and businesses. Without sufficient funding, roads would quickly
fall into disrepair, and society as we know it would grind to a halt.

I would also argue that increased government spending on railways is a bad idea at
this point in history. One reason why I take this view is the trend towards remote
working, which is likely to reduce the pressure on rail networks as commuter
numbers fall. I personally travel far less by train these days because I can work from
home, and many people are in a similar position. A second reason for my opinion is
the coming era of self-driving cars, which are predicted to revolutionise road
transport. I believe that people will increasingly choose to travel in driverless taxis
rather than on busy public trains.
