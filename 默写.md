the supplied illustrations explain the process of converting cocoa beans into liquid chocolate.

Looking from an overall perspective, it is readily apparent that there are  three main stages in this combination of a man-made and natural process, beginning with the collection and initial treatment of the beans, followed by transportation, culminating in the final processing in a factory.
The cocoa tree is grown in South America, Africa, and Indonesia and the pods are ripe for harvest when they turn red. The pods are then split open and the white cocoa beans are fermented under the leaves before being left in the sun to dry. In the subsequent stage, they are placed in large socks and sent by either train or truck to  factories for further preparations.
In the factory, The beans are roasted at 350 degrees Celsius prior to being crushed in a grinder, so that the outer shell can be separated out. The final step includes taking the inner part of the bean from the last step and pressing it into liquid chocolate.
